# Keep it safe


**What you'll do:** Understand the one secret that matters — your recovery phrase — and the handful of mistakes that drain wallets.

This is mainnet. Balances are real money, and on-chain transfers are irreversible. The threat model is simple: whoever holds your recovery phrase controls your funds.

---

## The recovery phrase is a root key

Your 12–24 words deterministically derive your wallet's private keys. Treat the phrase exactly as you'd treat a root private key or an unencrypted `id_rsa`.

**Do**
- Write it on paper and store it offline, ideally in two separate secure locations.
- Keep it out of any system that syncs to the cloud.

**Don't**
- Screenshot it, photograph it, or paste it into notes, chat, or email.
- Type it into any website. No legitimate site — TOSS included — ever asks for it.
- Store it in a general password manager unless it's a vault feature designed for seed phrases.

---

## PIN vs. phrase

The wallet's PIN/password locks the app on one device. The recovery phrase restores the wallet on any device. Losing the device is recoverable with the phrase; losing the phrase is not recoverable at all.

---

## What TOSS will and won't ask for

TOSS asks you to **approve transactions** in your wallet (connect, deploy, fund). It will **never** ask for your recovery phrase, PIN, or private key. Any prompt that does is a phishing attempt — stop and verify the domain.

---

## Verify what you sign

Before approving a sign prompt, glance at what it is: the asset, the amount, and the destination. For connecting a wallet to a bounty you'll approve an authorization; for funding you'll approve a USDC transfer with a visible network fee. If an amount or destination looks wrong, reject it.

<Callout type="warning">
On-chain transfers don't have an undo. There's no support line that can claw back funds sent to the wrong address or approved in a phishing flow. The checks above are the whole safety net.
</Callout>

---

## Recovering on a new device

Install the wallet, choose "Recover" (or "Import"), and enter your recovery phrase. The wallet and its balances reappear — which is exactly why the phrase must stay private.
