# 1. Find a bounty


**What you'll do:** Find an issue with a funded bounty you can take on.

---

## Where bounties live

A bounty is a GitHub issue labeled `rewarded` plus a tier (`low`/`medium`/`high`) or `manual`. The TOSS bot comments on each one confirming the reward amount and linking the on-chain escrow, so you can verify the money exists before you start.

Look for:

- The `rewarded` label plus a tier/`manual` label on the issue.
- A TOSS bot comment stating the reward in USDC with an escrow viewer link.
- Status `open` (unassigned) — an assigned bounty is already taken.

## Verify it's really funded

Because the escrow is on-chain, you don't have to take anyone's word for it. The bot's comment links the escrow in a viewer — open it and confirm the balance covers the reward. This is the point of a trustless bounty: proof before work.

---

## Pick something you can finish

Standard contributor judgment applies: read the issue and its discussion, check the acceptance criteria, and make sure the scope is clear. If anything's ambiguous, ask in the thread before requesting assignment — maintainers assign people who understand the work.

---

## Done when

You've found an open, bot-confirmed bounty whose scope you understand and whose escrow you've verified.
