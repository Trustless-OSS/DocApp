# Glossary

You know software. This is the vocabulary that's specific to Stellar, escrow, and the TOSS bounty model, defined plainly and linked from the pages that use them.

---

## Bounty

A reward in USDC attached to a GitHub issue. It becomes payable when the assigned contributor's merged PR closes the issue. Internally it's a record that reserves funds from the repo's escrow balance and tracks a status (`open` → `assigned` → `paid`/`cancelled`).

## Escrow

A per-repository smart contract on Stellar that holds the repo's USDC. Bounties reserve against its balance; releases, refunds, and splits are on-chain operations against it. Neither you nor the contributor can move the funds directly — the contract releases based on verified state.

## Milestone

The escrow's unit of payment for a single bounty. When a contributor connects a wallet, the bounty's milestone is pushed on-chain with an amount and a receiver address. Releasing a milestone pays the contributor; disputing and resolving one refunds the maintainer.

## USDC

A stablecoin pegged 1:1 to the US dollar, issued on Stellar by Circle. Bounties are denominated and paid in USDC. Your wallet must add a trustline before it can hold USDC.

## Trustline

A one-time, on-chain authorization that lets your Stellar account hold a specific asset — here, USDC. Without it, a USDC payout can't land in your wallet. It costs a tiny amount of XLM and persists until you remove it.

## XLM

Stellar's native asset (Lumens). Pays per-transaction network fees and satisfies the account's minimum reserve. You need a small balance to connect a wallet, add a trustline, deploy, or fund.

## Stellar

The blockchain TOSS settles on. Fast finality (a few seconds) and sub-cent fees. You interact with it only through your wallet's sign prompts.

## Mainnet

Stellar's production network, where balances are real money. These docs assume mainnet throughout.

## Recovery phrase (seed phrase)

The 12–24 words that deterministically derive your wallet's keys. Whoever holds them controls the funds. Treat them like a root private key: offline, never pasted into a site, never shared. TOSS never asks for them.

## Reward tier

A named `(label, amount)` pair stored on the repo. The defaults are `low`, `medium`, `high`, but tiers are fully editable — rename, delete, or add tiers mapped to any GitHub label. Labeling an issue with a tier applies that amount.

## Manual bounty

A bounty whose amount isn't a preset tier. You label the issue `rewarded` + `manual` and set the amount with `@trustless-oss <amount>` or by putting the amount in the issue body.

## The bot (`@trustless-oss`)

The GitHub App account that reacts to labels, assignments, merges, and comment commands. Mentioning `@trustless-oss` with a command triggers an action; it also posts status comments (bounty created, split saved, payout released).

## Split / partial payout

A pre-merge instruction (`@trustless-oss pay 70`) that divides the reward on merge: the contributor gets the percentage, the remainder returns to the maintainer. Stored as an intent; applied at release time.

## Dispute / reject

Rejecting work (`@trustless-oss reject` or the `rejected` label) disputes the milestone on-chain and resolves it back to the maintainer's wallet, refunding the reserved funds and cancelling the bounty.

## Closes #N

A GitHub keyword in a PR body that links it to an issue (`Closes`, `Fixes`, `Resolves`, and their variants all work). TOSS uses this link to match a merged PR to its bounty.
